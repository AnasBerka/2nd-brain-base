A robust categorization should balance **granularity (for analysis)** and **parsimony (for usability)**. In practice, 6–8 categories are optimal; beyond that, tracking noise outweighs insight.

Here is a widely accepted, analytically sound structure:

---

### 1. **Fixed Essentials**

**Definition:** Non-discretionary, recurring obligations.  
**Examples:** Rent, utilities, insurance, subscriptions (core), loan payments.  
**Role:** Baseline financial constraint; should be minimized relative to income.

---

### 2. **Variable Essentials**

**Definition:** Necessary but fluctuating expenses.  
**Examples:** Food, transportation, basic healthcare, school supplies.  
**Role:** Primary optimization target without harming quality of life.

---

### 3. **Financial Commitments**

**Definition:** Future-oriented allocations.  
**Examples:** Savings, investments, emergency fund, retirement contributions.  
**Role:** Determines long-term financial stability; should be treated as “non-negotiable.”

---

### 4. **Discretionary Spending**

**Definition:** Non-essential consumption.  
**Examples:** Eating out, entertainment, hobbies, shopping.  
**Role:** Main lever for short-term budget adjustment.

---

### 5. **Personal Development**

**Definition:** Skill and human capital investment.  
**Examples:** Courses, books, certifications, training.  
**Role:** High ROI category; often separated to avoid being cut prematurely.

---

### 6. **Social & Lifestyle**

**Definition:** Relationship and social obligations.  
**Examples:** Gifts, events, travel, family support.  
**Role:** Important for well-being but can drift upward if unbounded.

---

### 7. **Irregular / Unexpected**

**Definition:** Non-recurring or unpredictable costs.  
**Examples:** Repairs, medical emergencies, one-off fees.  
**Role:** Helps distinguish true overspending from randomness.

---

### Practical refinement

- Track at **category level**, not micro-items.
- Ensure categories are **mutually exclusive** and **collectively exhaustive**.
- Periodically compute each category as a **% of income** to assess materiality.

---

### Optional simplification (if you want minimal overhead)

You can collapse into 4 groups:

- Essentials (fixed + variable)
- Financial (savings/investments)
- Discretionary
- Irregular
